# Tool 2 Q12 Alignment

Tool 2 does not treat all Q12-style items as communication measures. It currently maps nine selected items because each has a plausible communication-related interpretation worth investigating. Q03, Q10, and Q12 remain intentionally unmapped. Mapping does not mean an item directly measures communication, and it does not mean communication caused the observed score.

The authoritative sources are the [communication rubric](../data/organizational/communication_rubric.csv) and [evidence validation matrix](../data/organizational/evidence_validation_matrix.csv). This document explains their current judgments; it adds no new research review or stronger validation claim. The matrix records automated preliminary claim-level reviews dated October 23, 2026, with human review pending. Targeted section checks are not comprehensive paper appraisals; abstract-only checks have additional limits.

## How to read the labels

- **Direct / partial / indirect** describe the conceptual relationship between item content and a communication discussion lens, not statistical confidence, effect size, or empirical validation. A direct conceptual link does **not** mean direct empirical validation.
- **Partially supported** means research supports part of the discussion framing, not the full mapping.
- **Indirect support** means research concerns a related construct, not the Q12 item itself.
- All rubric mappings retain `provisional_needs_evidence_review`. The matrix’s `validation_status` is a provisional review judgment, not a validated causal effect. Historical Gallup/overall support labels in the matrix are not confidence scores or analytical weights.

Survey results are signals and hypotheses only. They do not establish causes. No causal inference is performed. Neither mapping nor research context guarantees that a suggested action will improve a future Q12-style score.

## Item-by-item rationale and limits

### Q01 → Expectations & Direction

**Item content and Gallup context.** Q01 concerns knowing what is expected at work. Expanded communication interpretations remain provisional. [Gallup item context](https://www.gallup.com/q12-employee-engagement-survey/).

**Why communication is a discussion lens (direct).** The rubric focuses on clarity of expectations and work direction. Lower relative results may warrant investigating clarity of priorities, responsibilities, role expectations, and desired outcomes. This is a conceptual mapping, not a finding about the team’s cause or mechanism.

**What independent research supports.** Locke and Latham (2002) discusses goal specificity, feedback and task complexity. This supports exploring clear expectations, not diagnosing why Q01 is low. [Research source recorded in the matrix](https://goal-lab.psych.umn.edu/orgPsych/2020/readings/5.%20Motivation/Locke%20%26%20Latham%20%282002%29.pdf).

**What it does not support.** Goal-setting context does not measure role ownership or establish communication causation. Goal specificity alone does not ensure performance; task complexity and learning matter. This evidence does not independently validate the Tool 2 mapping or establish why the observed score occurred.

**Manager discussion framing.** Ask employees to describe expected outcomes and competing priorities. Clarify how responsibilities are understood without assuming poor communication caused the result.

**Review status and scope.** `partially_supported`; `targeted_full_text_sections`; `targeted_sections_checked`. Human review pending. Rubric status: `provisional_needs_evidence_review`.

### Q02 → Information & Resource Access

**Item content and Gallup context.** Public Gallup item wording concerns materials and equipment needed for work. Broader communication interpretations remain provisional. [Gallup item context](https://www.gallup.com/q12-employee-engagement-survey/).

**Why communication is a discussion lens (partial).** The rubric focuses on access to information and resources needed to perform work. Lower relative results may warrant investigating access to needed information, resources, workflow support, and operational barriers. This is a conceptual mapping, not a finding about the team’s cause or mechanism.

**What independent research supports.** Deng et al. (2021) treats accessible performance information as a job resource; its cross-sectional nonprofit sample does not test Q02 or diagnose resource barriers. [Research source recorded in the matrix](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.780718/full).

**What it does not support.** Materials and equipment are not information-access measures. The cross-sectional nonprofit study uses different resource measures and does not test Q02 or identify this team’s barriers. This evidence does not independently validate the Tool 2 mapping or establish why the observed score occurred.

**Manager discussion framing.** Ask which materials or tools are unavailable and whether access to information is also a concern. Do not assume the barrier is communication.

**Review status and scope.** `indirect_support`; `targeted_full_text_sections`; `targeted_sections_checked`. Human review pending. Rubric status: `provisional_needs_evidence_review`.

### Q04 → Recognition Communication

**Item content and Gallup context.** Q04 concerns recent recognition or praise for good work. Expanded communication interpretations remain provisional. [Gallup item context](https://www.gallup.com/q12-employee-engagement-survey/).

**Why communication is a discussion lens (direct).** The rubric focuses on recognition and reinforcement of valued work. Lower relative results may warrant investigating whether recognition is timely, meaningful, and communicates what work and behaviors are valued. This is a conceptual mapping, not a finding about the team’s cause or mechanism.

**What independent research supports.** Bradler et al. (2014 working paper) experimentally studies unexpected public recognition in a short data-entry job. It supports recognition as a topic, not claims about preferred timing or meaningfulness for this team. [Research source recorded in the matrix](https://docs.iza.org/dp8311.pdf).

**What it does not support.** A short data-entry experiment with mainly student workers does not establish this team’s recognition preferences, preferred timing, or mechanisms. It does not justify ranking or publicly rewarding employees. This evidence does not independently validate the Tool 2 mapping or establish why the observed score occurred.

**Manager discussion framing.** Ask what recognition employees receive and how they experience it. Explore timing and meaning as questions, not inferred deficiencies.

**Review status and scope.** `partially_supported`; `targeted_full_text_sections`; `targeted_sections_checked`. Human review pending. Rubric status: `provisional_needs_evidence_review`.

### Q05 → Relational Support

**Item content and Gallup context.** Public Gallup item wording concerns perceived care from a supervisor or another person at work. Broader communication interpretations remain provisional. [Gallup item context](https://www.gallup.com/q12-employee-engagement-survey/).

**Why communication is a discussion lens (indirect).** The rubric focuses on perceived care and interpersonal support. Lower relative results may warrant investigating perceived care, respect, responsiveness, and interpersonal support. This is a conceptual mapping, not a finding about the team’s cause or mechanism.

**What independent research supports.** Rhoades and Eisenberger (2002) links supervisor support with perceived organizational support. The construct concerns organizational care and is not equivalent to Q05 interpersonal care or responsiveness. [Research source recorded in the matrix](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12184574/).

**What it does not support.** Organizational support is broader than interpersonal care. Respect and responsiveness are follow-up topics, not measured mechanisms. Only the indexed abstract was reviewed; full-text appraisal is pending. This evidence does not independently validate the Tool 2 mapping or establish why the observed score occurred.

**Manager discussion framing.** Ask how employees experience care and support at work. Explore respect or responsiveness only through follow-up discussion.

**Review status and scope.** `indirect_support`; `abstract_only`; `access_pending`. Human review pending. Rubric status: `provisional_needs_evidence_review`.

### Q06 → Developmental Communication

**Item content and Gallup context.** Q06 concerns someone encouraging development at work. Expanded communication interpretations remain provisional. [Gallup item context](https://www.gallup.com/q12-employee-engagement-survey/).

**Why communication is a discussion lens (direct).** The rubric focuses on coaching and encouragement of development. Lower relative results may warrant investigating coaching, developmental feedback, career support, and encouragement to grow. This is a conceptual mapping, not a finding about the team’s cause or mechanism.

**What independent research supports.** Allen et al. (2004) synthesizes mentoring and career outcomes. Career and psychosocial mentoring provide context for development support, but do not validate Q06 as a coaching-quality or feedback measure. [Research source recorded in the matrix](https://www.same.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/career-benefits-associated-with-mentoring-for-proteges.pdf).

**What it does not support.** Mentoring includes more than communication. Mostly nonexperimental studies limit causal ordering; Q06 does not measure coaching quality or developmental feedback, and objective outcome associations were small. This evidence does not independently validate the Tool 2 mapping or establish why the observed score occurred.

**Manager discussion framing.** Ask who encourages development and which forms of support employees find useful. Explore coaching and career conversations without assuming their absence.

**Review status and scope.** `partially_supported`; `targeted_full_text_sections`; `targeted_sections_checked`. Human review pending. Rubric status: `provisional_needs_evidence_review`.

### Q07 → Employee Voice

**Item content and Gallup context.** Public Gallup item wording concerns whether employee opinions are perceived to count. Broader communication interpretations remain provisional. [Gallup item context](https://www.gallup.com/q12-employee-engagement-survey/).

**Why communication is a discussion lens (direct).** The rubric focuses on whether employees feel their opinions are heard and valued. Lower relative results may warrant investigating whether employees feel heard, have opportunities to contribute ideas, and see follow-up on their input. This is a conceptual mapping, not a finding about the team’s cause or mechanism.

**What independent research supports.** Morrison (2014) defines upward voice as voluntary communication of ideas or concerns to higher organizational positions. This supports exploring input, but does not establish that Q07 measures voice behavior or follow-up. [Research source recorded in the matrix](https://www.annualreviews.org/content/journals/10.1146/annurev-orgpsych-031413-091328).

**What it does not support.** Q07 does not directly measure voice behavior, psychological safety, or actual follow-through. Perceived input efficacy is a possible discussion topic requiring separate evidence. This evidence does not independently validate the Tool 2 mapping or establish why the observed score occurred.

**Manager discussion framing.** Ask whether employees feel their opinions are considered. Separately explore opportunities to speak and what happens after input.

**Review status and scope.** `partially_supported`; `targeted_full_text_sections`; `targeted_sections_checked`. Human review pending. Rubric status: `provisional_needs_evidence_review`.

### Q08 → Purpose Alignment

**Item content and Gallup context.** Public Gallup item wording concerns whether organizational purpose makes the job feel important. Broader communication interpretations remain provisional. [Gallup item context](https://www.gallup.com/q12-employee-engagement-survey/).

**Why communication is a discussion lens (direct).** The rubric focuses on connection between work and organizational mission or purpose. Lower relative results may warrant investigating whether employees understand how their work connects to organizational mission and purpose. This is a conceptual mapping, not a finding about the team’s cause or mechanism.

**What independent research supports.** Grant (2008) studies task significance through three field experiments. Beneficiary impact and social worth offer context for discussing meaningful work, but do not validate Q08 mission alignment or a communication mechanism. [Research source recorded in the matrix](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18211139/).

**What it does not support.** Beneficiary impact and social worth differ from organizational mission alignment. Controlled cues, possible diffusion, and young samples constrain transfer; a messaging failure cannot be inferred. This evidence does not independently validate the Tool 2 mapping or establish why the observed score occurred.

**Manager discussion framing.** Ask how the organizational purpose relates to the importance employees experience in their work. Explore understanding without assuming a messaging failure.

**Review status and scope.** `indirect_support`; `targeted_full_text_sections`; `targeted_sections_checked`. Human review pending. Rubric status: `provisional_needs_evidence_review`.

### Q09 → Team Norms & Coordination

**Item content and Gallup context.** Public Gallup item wording concerns coworker commitment to quality work. Broader communication interpretations remain provisional. [Gallup item context](https://www.gallup.com/q12-employee-engagement-survey/).

**Why communication is a discussion lens (indirect).** The rubric focuses on shared commitment to quality and team standards. Lower relative results may warrant investigating shared quality standards, coordination, handoffs, and team expectations. This is a conceptual mapping, not a finding about the team’s cause or mechanism.

**What independent research supports.** Havens et al. (2010) reports an association between relational coordination and nurse-reported care quality. It supports discussing coordination in context, not treating Q09 as a coordination or handoff measure. [Research source recorded in the matrix](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21073566/).

**What it does not support.** Coworker commitment to quality is not itself a measure of coordination or handoff quality. The healthcare-specific self-report association does not establish handoff failures or causal direction. Only the indexed abstract was reviewed; full-text appraisal is pending. This evidence does not independently validate the Tool 2 mapping or establish why the observed score occurred.

**Manager discussion framing.** Ask what commitment to quality looks like in daily work. Explore shared standards or handoffs only as possible explanations requiring additional evidence.

**Review status and scope.** `indirect_support`; `abstract_only`; `access_pending`. Human review pending. Rubric status: `provisional_needs_evidence_review`.

### Q11 → Progress Feedback

**Item content and Gallup context.** Q11 concerns having a progress conversation in the preceding six months. Expanded communication interpretations remain provisional. [Gallup item context](https://www.gallup.com/q12-employee-engagement-survey/).

**Why communication is a discussion lens (direct).** The rubric focuses on conversations about progress and performance. Lower relative results may warrant investigating the frequency, clarity, and usefulness of conversations about progress and performance. This is a conceptual mapping, not a finding about the team’s cause or mechanism.

**What independent research supports.** Kluger and DeNisi (1996) finds feedback interventions have variable performance effects, including negative effects. This supports exploring feedback experiences, not assuming more frequent conversations improve outcomes. [Research source recorded in the matrix](https://cris.huji.ac.il/en/publications/the-effects-of-feedback-interventions-on-performance-a-historical/).

**What it does not support.** Occurrence of a progress conversation does not establish its clarity or usefulness. Feedback interventions differ from progress conversations and can have negative effects. Only the author-university abstract was reviewed; full-text access remains pending. This evidence does not independently validate the Tool 2 mapping or establish why the observed score occurred.

**Manager discussion framing.** Ask what progress conversations cover and whether employees find them useful. Do not infer conversation quality from occurrence or prescribe increased frequency alone.

**Review status and scope.** `partially_supported`; `abstract_only`; `access_pending`. Human review pending. Rubric status: `provisional_needs_evidence_review`.

## Why Q03, Q10, and Q12 are not currently mapped

The current Tool 2 communication rubric does not contain validated or sufficiently defensible communication mappings for Q03 (opportunities to use strengths), Q10 (close workplace relationships), or Q12 (learning and growth). They can still appear in general engagement strengths and areas for investigation, but are not converted into communication diagnostic dimensions. This is a boundary of the current rubric, not a claim that these topics have no relationship with communication in reality. The nine included mappings also remain provisional, rather than validated communication measures.

## Product boundary and historical wording

Historical judge wording about “six selected Q12 questions” is ambiguous. The current Tool 2 rubric contains nine mapped items, all documented here; no reduction to six is authorized. The README’s older “six dimensions” refers to a Tool 1 communication rubric, not Tool 2’s Q12 mappings.

This explanation does not change item means, GrandMean, hierarchy scores, strengths/opportunities, signal ordering, privacy thresholds, suppression, Survey-to-Action selection, or the manager discussion workflow. Managers retain responsibility for deciding what to investigate and whether to agree an action.
